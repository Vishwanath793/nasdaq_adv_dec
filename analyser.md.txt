4. BUILD A COMPREHENSIVE VOLUME FEATURE ENGINE

Compute indicators locally from raw trades or OHLCV whenever possible. Do not waste free API calls fetching indicators that can be calculated from existing data.

Prioritize the following feature families.

A. Absolute participation
- Cumulative pre-market shares.
- Cumulative pre-market dollar turnover.
- Last 1/5/15/30-minute volume.
- Last 1/5/15/30-minute dollar turnover.
- Trade count if supplied.
- Average trade size if supported.
- Number and proportion of active intervals.
- Time since the last eligible trade.

Dollar turnover:
- Prefer sum(price × size) from eligible trades.
- Otherwise use actual bar VWAP × bar volume if available.
- If using close × volume, label it an approximation.

B. Same-time relative participation
- Cumulative same-time pre-market RVOL.
- Rolling-window RVOL.
- Same-time relative dollar turnover.
- Opening-five-minute RVOL.
- Historical percentile of participation.
- Robust volume surprise relative to median/MAD where appropriate.

Use:
RVOL(t) =
today’s eligible volume from session start through t
/
historical baseline volume over the identical session/time interval.

Require the same:
- Feed scope.
- Session definition.
- Trade-condition policy.
- Adjustment basis.
- Clock-time window.

Never divide current IEX volume by historical consolidated SIP volume and call the result RVOL.

Use 20 comparable completed sessions as an initial target, configurable.
Report the actual sample size.
Handle missing history and genuine zero-volume sessions separately.
Do not create huge ratios from tiny denominators.

C. Volume acceleration
- Recent volume per minute versus the preceding window.
- Recent activity versus the historical same-clock-time expectation.
- Change in volume rate.
- Whether activity is expanding, steady, or fading.
- Participation immediately after a catalyst.
- Persistence across multiple intervals.

Normalize for the natural increase in activity near the open.
A rise from 08:00 to 09:25 should not automatically be called unusual acceleration.

D. Volume persistence and concentration
- Fraction of intervals with meaningful activity.
- Longest active sequence.
- Fraction of volume in the largest interval.
- Fraction concentrated in a few prints, where available.
- Sustained participation versus a one-print spike.
- Activity persistence after the initial price jump.

A huge print followed by silence should not receive the same conviction as sustained participation.

E. Price–volume agreement
Evaluate separately for long and short setups:
- Rising price with expanding participation.
- Falling price with expanding participation.
- Progress in the setup direction per unit of activity.
- Volume during directional pushes.
- Volume during countertrend pullbacks.
- Pullback-volume contraction.
- Recovery-volume expansion.
- High-volume rejection.
- High-volume failure to make further progress.
- Breakout volume and follow-through.
- Breakdown volume and follow-through.
- Failed breaks returning into the previous range.

Volume measures activity, not direction by itself.

F. VWAP and volume-supported acceptance
- Session VWAP.
- VWAP slope.
- Price distance from VWAP.
- Duration above/below VWAP.
- Share of traded volume above/below VWAP where measurable.
- VWAP reclaim/rejection with participation.
- Repeated VWAP crossings as a choppiness measure.
- Price acceptance beyond pre-market highs/lows.
- Volume sustained after crossing a key level.

Separate pre-market VWAP from regular-session VWAP.

G. Volume distribution by price
Where trade-level data permits:
- Volume-at-price.
- Point of control.
- Value-area estimates.
- High/low-volume regions.
- Migration of the volume distribution.

With only OHLCV bars, mark any volume profile as estimated.
Do not pretend to know exactly where inside a candle its volume traded.

H. Float and capacity context
Where reliable dated data exists:
- Volume divided by float.
- Dollar turnover relative to comparable stocks.
- Typical dollar liquidity.
- Proposed order size as a share of observed activity.
- Liquidity relative to intended position size.

If only shares outstanding is known, label turnover against shares outstanding accordingly.

Low float and high turnover can indicate execution risk as well as opportunity.
Do not automatically reward them.

I. Historical volume context
- Previous-day RVOL.
- Previous-session volume trend.
- Volume around recent gaps.
- Volume on prior breakouts/rejections.
- Historical opening-window participation.
- Earnings versus non-earnings participation.
- Typical follow-through following comparable volume shocks.

J. Secondary research indicators
Optionally calculate:
- OBV.
- Money Flow Index.
- Chaikin Money Flow.
- Accumulation/distribution measures.
- Volume-price trend.

These are derived transformations, not independent confirmations.
Keep them in a capped research family until validated.

5. DO NOT INVENT BUY/SELL VOLUME

If the data contains trades and contemporaneous quotes, implement a documented aggressor-classification estimate where feasible.

Expose:
- Estimated buy-initiated volume.
- Estimated sell-initiated volume.
- Unclassified volume.
- Classification method and coverage.

With OHLCV only:
- “Up-bar volume” is not actual buy volume.
- “Down-bar volume” is not actual sell volume.
- A candle-based delta is a proxy, not true order flow.

Never claim:
- Institutional accumulation.
- Smart-money buying.
- Hidden buying.
- Real cumulative volume delta.

Unless the underlying data genuinely supports the specific claim.

6. VOLUME-FIRST SCORE

Create an initial configurable research preset:

- Relative participation: 20%.
- Participation acceleration and persistence: 15%.
- Price–volume agreement: 15%.
- VWAP/volume-supported acceptance: 10%.
- Price structure and gap context: 15%.
- Market and sector alignment: 10%.
- Verified catalyst: 10%.
- Historical setup context: 5%.

The first four groups allocate 60% to volume-related evidence.

These weights are hypotheses, not validated optimal weights.
Retain an equal-group baseline for comparison.

Liquidity, stale data, halted status, and execution feasibility remain separate eligibility gates. A high score must not override them.

Within groups:
- Cap correlated contributions.
- Avoid rewarding shares, dollar turnover, trade count, and RVOL repeatedly for the same underlying activity.
- Use bounded transformations.
- Normalize against appropriate liquidity/size cohorts.
- Avoid ranking every stock against mega-cap absolute volume.

Volume alone must never decide long versus short.
Combine it with strategy direction and price response.

Missing mandatory volume data must prevent a “strong volume conviction” label.
Do not reallocate all missing volume weight to news and gap size.

Keep:
- Raw score.
- Volume evidence score.
- Data coverage.
- Source quality.
- Tradeability.
- Final decision.

Separate free-data profiles when comparing rankings.

7. DISTINGUISH CONTINUATION FROM EXHAUSTION

Explicitly evaluate opposing interpretations:

Possible continuation:
- Elevated same-time RVOL.
- Sustained activity across intervals.
- Price progress in the setup direction.
- Controlled pullbacks on reduced volume.
- Recovery/breakout on renewed volume.
- Stable executable liquidity.

Possible exhaustion or failure:
- Extreme volume with little price progress.
- Large rejection wick.
- Failed breakout on heavy activity.
- Sharp volume spike followed by inactivity.
- Excessive distance from VWAP.
- Widening spreads.
- Countertrend volume overwhelming the recovery.

Treat these as research patterns requiring validation.
Do not mechanically reward every increase in volume.
